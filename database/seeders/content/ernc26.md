### Conference Overview

The Esports Research Network Conference 2026 (ERNC26) took place from **15 to 17 September 2026** at the **University of Tsukuba**, in the University's 30th Anniversary Hall (Daigaku Kaikan). After the fully online edition of 2025, ERNC26 brought the network back into one room, and it did so in a country that shaped the history of games like few others. Japan is a gaming powerhouse with an unparalleled heritage in console and arcade gaming; its esports ecosystem, however, has only recently started to grow, helped by the relaxation of prize money regulations in 2018 and the rise of professional leagues. Consequently, Tsukuba was a fitting place to ask what gaming heritage means for the future of esports.

The conference was hosted by the University of Tsukuba, funded by the KDDI Foundation and the Tsukuba Tourism and Convention Association, and co-organised by GAKU.

### Theme and Contributions

ERNC26 was organised under the theme **“Bridging Gaming Heritage and Esports Futures: Transformations, Traditions, and Tomorrow”**, with a particular focus on intercultural perspectives and on the relationship between Asia and other regions. The programme reflected the breadth of the field. Health and performance science was strongly represented, from physical conditioning and cardiovascular reactivity to exercise adherence and the menstrual cycle in training and competition. Governance and integrity formed a second cluster, with work on legal frameworks, e-doping, AI ethics and the role of the state. Further sessions examined fandom across borders, collegiate esports, inclusion, older adults as players, and esports tourism.

Three keynotes framed the programme: **Hideki Okamura** (SEGA, JESU and GAKU) on the esports journey in Japan, **Brian McCauley** (Jönköping International Business School) on gaming heritage as infrastructure for esports and the digital society, and **Michael Murray** (Bandai Namco Entertainment) on keeping community at the heart of esports. An opening panel with SEGA, KONAMI Esports Academy and the Japan Esports Union discussed how publishers and public authorities shape Japan's esports scene.

### Format and Participation

ERNC26 brought together **163 participants from 29 countries** and more than 40 institutions. The Book of Abstracts contains **64 peer-reviewed presentations and one panel**, held in four parallel session rooms alongside the keynotes and industry panels. In line with ERN practice, the plenary sessions were streamed on the ERN Twitch channel and the recordings are published on the ERN YouTube channel.
